The maths
raw mass = guarded final mass ×
(target fraction ÷ effective retention)

Charge allowance adds material proportionally; it does not protect fineness. The guard raises the final precious-metal target, then the retention model back-calculates the raw charge. For gold, carat is Au fraction × 24; for platinum, use Pt parts per thousand.

Melt path notes

Heat with intent.

Gold is forgiving compared with platinum, but both reward clean charge material, a dedicated crucible and a controlled atmosphere. Treat these as route cards, then follow your equipment and alloy supplier instructions.

01 / MACHINEG1 gold · Fusus Pt

In the machine crucible

Best repeatability for jewellery casting when the machine, crucible and programme are matched to the metal. For platinum, think Pt-capable centrifugal equipment such as a Galloni Fusus class machine, not a standard gold G1 cycle.

  • Confirm the exact Galloni model, kit and approved temperature range.
  • Charge clean, dry, known material without packing it tight.
  • For gold, keep volatile zinc-bearing additions late and controlled.
  • For platinum, use a Pt-rated ceramic or quartz crucible and controlled atmosphere; pre-alloying can be cast into small buttons / ingots for the next charge.
02 / PRE-ALLOYoutside the casting cycle

Make a known button

Useful when you want a homogeneous house alloy before it becomes a production charge.

  • Weigh each raw metal or certified master alloy separately.
  • Use a clean, dedicated, temperature-rated setup and a suitable atmosphere.
  • Make a button or ingot, cool safely, then shot / granulate / roll before charging.
  • Record melt loss and verify the finished alloy if the batch matters.
03 / TORCHsmall batch · trained operator

Bench melt

Good for small gold work when the operator can control the flame; not the default route for production platinum.

  • Use a dedicated crucible, dry tools, heat / face protection and local exhaust.
  • Keep the flame moving; avoid long overheating that drives off zinc or oxidises additions.
  • Introduce volatile additions through a suitable master alloy, not loose powder into a live puddle.
  • For platinum, use only a purpose-built Pt torch setup and trained procedure; never improvise with a carbon-rich fuel or graphite.

Non-negotiables

Molten metal and fumes are process hazards. Ventilation, training and equipment condition are part of the recipe.

Nickel white goldCan sensitise skin. Check the exact alloy and local product requirements; choose Pd-white when appropriate.
Zinc / volatile metalsCan fume or shift composition when overheated. Use local exhaust and supplier guidance.
Cadmium / berylliumNot included in this calculator. Do not add them to a jewellery melt without specialist controls.
Platinum temperaturePt is a high-temperature process. A gold-only machine, crucible or torch is not enough.

Temperature is not a recipe

Pure-metal melting points are only a starting reference. Each alloy has a melting range, superheat requirement, atmosphere sensitivity and mould interaction. Use the alloy supplier's casting sheet and the machine's stored programme.

Gold: a Galloni G1 listing describes a type-S temperature system up to about 1,450 °C.

Platinum: use the appropriate Pt-capable Galloni Fusus / platinum system and ceramic or quartz crucible; listings are around 2,100 °C. Confirm your exact machine before charging Pt.
Reference & provenance

Know the numbers.

Carat is a gold convention; platinum is reported by fineness. Market labels are not the same thing as an independent assay or a legal hallmark.
